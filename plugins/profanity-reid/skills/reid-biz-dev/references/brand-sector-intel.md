# Brand Sector Intelligence

Reference for understanding how outdoor, lifestyle, and action sports brand teams are structured, how they make creative decisions, and where Profanity fits.

---

## Outdoor Brands

**Who they are:** REI, Patagonia, Arc'teryx, Black Diamond, Osprey, Mystery Ranch, Cotopaxi, Kühl, Fjällräven, Salomon, La Sportiva, Mammut, Gregory, NEMO, Big Agnes, MSR, Jetboil, Goal Zero

**Brand culture:** Mission-driven. Sustainability and authenticity are table stakes, not differentiators. Larger players (Patagonia, Arc'teryx) have strong in-house creative. Mid-tier brands often have lean teams with big ambitions and constrained production budgets.

**How creative decisions get made:**
- At large brands: Brand Creative Director or VP Marketing owns the creative direction. Senior Designer or Art Director may be the day-to-day contact. Budget typically approved by VP or CMO.
- At mid-size brands: Marketing Director or Brand Manager wears multiple hats. They're often stretched — overseeing campaigns, shoots, content, retail assets simultaneously. A creative partner who reduces their cognitive load is genuinely valuable.
- At small/emerging brands: Founder or co-founder is often the creative decision-maker. Move fast, value scrappiness and speed.

**What they spend on visual content:** Seasonal gear launches, campaign imagery, e-commerce product shots, retail POS, wholesale sell-in decks, trade show materials. 3D is increasingly used for gear that's hard to photograph (technical backpacks, complex layering systems, detailed hardware).

**Pain points:**
- Shoot logistics for gear that hasn't been manufactured yet (3D solves this)
- Inconsistent product rendering across markets and platforms
- Needing studio-quality imagery for a SKU that doesn't justify a full shoot budget
- Pre-sell and sell-in materials needed months before product exists

**Where they hang out:**
- Outdoor Retailer (Salt Lake City, Jan + June) — the industry's flagship trade show
- Outdoor Retailer Snow Show (used to be separate, now consolidated)
- Backpacker Retailer (smaller regional)
- Grassroots Outdoor Alliance (more wholesale/retail focused)
- Conservation Alliance events
- Instagram, LinkedIn for brand marketers

**Notes on approach:**
- Lead with craft and attention to material detail — outdoor consumers are knowledgeable, brand teams know they'll notice when renders look fake
- Reference environmental context and how product photography captures the ethos of use
- Don't lean too hard on sustainability messaging unless you've genuinely integrated it into Profanity's operations

## Lifestyle Brands

**Who they are:** Hydro Flask, Yeti, Stanley (post-viral), Kavu, Topo Designs, United By Blue, Danner, Carhartt, Filson, Pendleton, Tillys, Grown Alchemist, Aesop-adjacents, apparel brands with outdoor crossover (Vuori, Public Rec, Alo, Ten Thousand)

**Brand culture:** Aesthetic-first, community-oriented, often blurs line between outdoor and fashion. Visual identity is everything. Brand equity is fragile and brand teams protect it fiercely.

**How creative decisions get made:**
- Creative Director or Head of Brand owns the vision. Social content and e-comm teams often have separate budgets and different priorities.
- At DTC-heavy brands: Performance marketing and brand marketing are often siloed. DTC teams want assets that convert; brand teams want assets that feel right. Profanity can serve both.
- Approval chains can be long. Get allies in-house before pitching upward.

**What they spend on visual content:** Hero campaign imagery, lifestyle product photography, e-comm / PDP images, social content, seasonal lookbooks, email assets, paid social. Growing use of 3D for DTC brands that need high-volume SKU imagery without per-shoot costs.

**Pain points:**
- Volume problem: too many SKUs, colorways, and variants to shoot everything
- Speed: need assets before products arrive, especially for DTC pre-orders
- Consistency: product looks different across every touch point
- Cost: shooting everything in-context (lifestyle environments) is prohibitively expensive at scale

**Where they hang out:**
- Magic (Las Vegas, Feb + Aug) — lifestyle apparel trade show
- Agenda (LB + NYC) — streetwear/surf/skate adjacent
- The Accessories Show
- Designers + Art Directors community spaces: It's Nice That, Dribbble, Are.na, Brand New
- LinkedIn (brand directors are active here)
- Instagram (follow their brand accounts, engage thoughtfully)

**Notes on approach:**
- These brands care intensely about their visual world. Before reaching out, understand their aesthetic. What photographers do they tag? What's the visual language of their latest campaign?
- Pitch the consistency and scalability of 3D — not just quality. A lifestyle brand managing 200 SKUs a season is drowning in shot lists.
- Social proof matters here: if Profanity has done work for a recognizable lifestyle brand, lead with it.

## Action Sports Brands

**Who they are:** Burton, Lib Tech, GNU, Salomon (snow), K2, Jones Snowboards, Arbor, Capita, Rome, Smith Optics, Dragon Alliance, Oakley, Spy, Dakine, Volcom, Quiksilver, Billabong, Roxy, O'Neill, Rip Curl, FCS, Channel Islands, JS Industries, Santa Cruz (skate), Powell Peralta, Thrasher, Independent, Vans, DC Shoes, etnies

**Brand culture:** Authenticity and credibility in the community are non-negotiable. Brands that feel corporate get dragged. Creative teams often came up through the sport — they respect craft and catch inauthenticity immediately.

**How creative decisions get made:**
- Creative Director or Brand Manager, often with direct founder/athlete input at mid-size brands
- Endorsement from the team riders or sponsored athletes can carry enormous weight internally
- Season-driven: snow brands work on Aug–Oct timelines for winter, spring brands April–June
- Board sports brands often have tight relationships with longtime agencies or in-house teams — breaking in requires a compelling reason to try someone new

**What they spend on visual content:** Team rider content, product hero shots, gear campaigns, apparel lookbooks, web and e-comm assets. Snowboard/ski hardgoods (boards, bindings, skis, helmets) are particularly well-suited for 3D given complex graphics and construction details.

**Pain points:**
- Graphic-heavy products (snowboard bases, decks, helmets) need hero imagery that can showcase intricate designs — 3D allows infinite angles, zero limitations
- Pre-season sell-in: product needs to be presented to retailers before it's manufactured
- Action photography is expensive and hard to control; product-focused 3D as a complement to lifestyle shoots
- Small brand teams doing the work of much larger departments

**Where they hang out:**
- SIA (Snow Sports) — now part of OR Snow Show
- Surf Expo (Orlando, Jan + Sept)
- ISPO (Munich, Nov) — European action sports and outdoor
- Agenda Trade Show (Long Beach)
- Snowboarder Mag / Transworld events
- Brand teams follow each other on Instagram religiously

**Notes on approach:**
- Know the brand's athletes and recent team activity before reaching out
- Reference their graphics program or product design language — shows you actually looked
- Action sports teams are small and loyal; if you can get one person to champion Profanity internally, they'll move the whole thing
- Don't pitch the tech first — pitch the outcome for their product

## Decision-Maker Titles to Target

| Role | What they care about | How to approach |
|------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Creative Director | The work looks right, brand equity | Lead with aesthetic credibility and a specific reaction to their work |
| Brand Manager / Director | Campaigns land on time and budget | Lead with efficiency, reliability, and strategic partnership |
| VP Marketing / CMO | Revenue, brand momentum, ROI | Lead with business outcomes; let them pull the creative details out |
| Senior Designer / Art Director | Technical quality, ease of working together | Lead with the collaboration — how Profanity works, not just what it produces |
| Founder | Speed, trust, results | Short, direct, social proof. They don't have time for anything else. |

## Signals a Brand Is Ready for a Conversation

- Recent rebrand or new CD/brand director hire (change = opportunity)
- New product line or category expansion
- DTC push or major e-comm overhaul
- Recent funding announcement (Series A/B for emerging brands)
- Seasonal catalog in production (sell-in materials are time-sensitive)
- Job posting for a 3D artist, motion designer, or production coordinator (signals need)
- Low-quality product imagery currently live (they know it, they hate it)
