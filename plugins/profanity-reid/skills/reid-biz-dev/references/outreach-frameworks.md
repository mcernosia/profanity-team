# Outreach Frameworks

Research process, email structures, and principles for outreach that doesn't feel like outreach.

---

## The Pre-Outreach Research Checklist

Before writing a single word, know these things about the brand:

**Brand identity:**
- What is their aesthetic language right now? (Review IG, website, recent campaigns)
- What campaign or launch have they put out recently that's worth referencing?
- Who are they speaking to? What does their customer look like?
- What's the brand's current cultural moment — growing, pivoting, repositioning?

**Visual content:**
- What does their current product photography look like? (Honest quality assessment)
- Are they using 3D anywhere? How good is it?
- Where are they inconsistent or underserving their brand visually?
- What's a specific product or category that could benefit from 3D?

**Business signals:**
- Any recent news: funding, new product line, rebrand, leadership change?
- Are they hiring? (LinkedIn job posts are biz dev gold — a 3D artist posting = they have demand but no solution yet)
- What trade shows do they do? Did they just exhibit somewhere?

**The contact:**
- Who specifically should you reach out to? (See decision-maker guide in brand-sector-intel.md)
- Have they posted anything recently you can reference?
- Do they have any mutual connections or follow any of the same accounts?

**The hook:**
- What is the ONE specific observation about their brand that will make this message feel non-generic?
- What problem can you name that will make them think "how did they know?"

If you don't have a hook, don't send the email yet. Go find one.

## Email Structure: The OVNQ Framework

Four-part structure for cold outreach that earns attention:

**O — Observation**
Start with something specific and real about their brand. Not a compliment — an observation. The difference: a compliment is "I love your work." An observation is "The texture work on the Ridgeline backpack in your fall campaign is the kind of thing that makes outdoor consumers stop scrolling."

**V — Value Signal**
One sentence that establishes Profanity's relevance to their world — without pitching. Example: "We do 3D product visualization for outdoor and lifestyle brands that need studio-quality imagery before gear ships."

**N — Name the Problem (optional but powerful)**
If the research revealed an obvious gap (inconsistent product imagery, low-quality renders, visible shoot limitations), name it directly. "Noticed your [product] imagery on the PDP feels like it's fighting against the quality of the product itself." This feels risky but lands extremely well when it's accurate.

**Q — One Question**
End with a single open question that invites a response without demanding a meeting. Examples:
- "Curious whether [specific problem] is something you're thinking about heading into next season."
- "Is that something your team is handling in-house right now?"
- "Would it be worth a conversation before your [fall/spring] campaign kicks off?"

Not: "Would love to jump on a call to show you what we do." That's a vendor pitch. Ask a question that a peer would ask.

## Subject Lines That Get Opened

**Principles:**
- Reference them, not you
- Specific beats clever
- Curious beats promotional
- Short (under 8 words)

**Patterns that work:**
- "[Brand name] x Profanity — quick thought" (signals personal, not mass)
- "Re: [specific product or campaign name]" (looks like a reply, earns the open)
- "3D for [product category] — quick question" (direct, specific)
- "[Observation as a subject]" — e.g., "The Ridgeline PDP" or "Your fall launch" (creates curiosity)

**Patterns that don't work:**
- "Exploring a collaboration" (generic)
- "Profanity Creative — Award-Winning 3D Studio" (vendor mode)
- "Following up on..." as a cold opener (you've never talked)
- Question marks in cold subject lines (feels like clickbait)

## Follow-Up Message Principles

**When to follow up:**
- First follow-up: 5–7 business days after initial outreach, if no response
- Second follow-up: 7–10 days after first follow-up
- Third and final: Only if there was a prior positive signal (they opened, engaged, or met briefly)

**Structure:**
- Keep it shorter than the original
- Don't re-pitch — add a new piece of value or reference something new (recent launch, industry moment, something relevant that happened)
- One question. Not one ask — one question.
- If it's the final follow-up, name it: "I'll leave it here — happy to reconnect whenever the timing is right."

**What not to say:**
- "Just checking in" — signals you have nothing new to offer
- "I know you're busy, but..." — self-deprecating, weakens your position
- "Did you get a chance to look at this?" — makes them feel guilty
- Any version of desperation or urgency you're manufacturing

## The Warm Outreach Adjustment

If you have any connection to this person — mutual contact, met at OR, they liked a post, you worked with someone at their old company — open with it. One sentence. It changes everything.

"[Mutual contact] mentioned you've been revamping your product line heading into next season."

"Caught your panel at OR in June — the thing you said about [specific topic] stuck with me."

"Your DM on the [project name] work was appreciated — wanted to follow up properly."

Warm context converts at dramatically higher rates and shortens the sales cycle. If you have it, use it first.

## Channels and Sequencing

**Primary channel: Email.** Most brand decision-makers manage their business through email. LinkedIn is increasingly noisy. Cold calls are dead in creative.

**LinkedIn DM:** Use only if you can't find their email or if you've engaged meaningfully with their content first (liked/commented over a period of weeks). Don't pitch in a DM — ask to take a conversation to email.

**Instagram:** Follow the brand and the individual. Engage authentically with their content over time. Only reach out via IG DM if you have a specific, short, non-pitchy reason to do so. "Your packaging rollout looks incredible — happy to connect if you're ever thinking about how 3D could extend it." That's a DM. A full pitch is not.

**The sequence:**
1. Follow and engage (Instagram/LinkedIn) — 2–4 weeks of genuine engagement
2. Email outreach (primary)
3. LinkedIn DM (if no email, or as a warm follow)
4. Instagram DM (only if established authentic presence)

**Never:** Reach out on multiple channels simultaneously with the same message. It reads as spam and destroys the relationship before it starts.

## Outreach for Warm Leads vs. Cold Prospects

**Warm lead (met them, got referral, previous interest):**
- Reference the shared context immediately
- Skip the credibility-building — they're already interested
- Go faster: propose a specific next step in the first message instead of ending with a question
- "Based on what we talked about at OR, I wanted to send over [specific thing]. Are you free for 20 minutes next week to dig in?"

**Cold prospect (never interacted):**
- The OVNQ framework (above)
- Slower — earn the right to a conversation before asking for one
- No attachments, no portfolio links unless they're hyper-relevant to a specific hook
- Goal of first message: a response, not a meeting

---

## What a Good Response Looks Like (and How to Capitalize on It)

If they respond — even with "We're not looking for anything right now" — you have a live lead.

**For a positive response:**
- Respond within 24 hours. Momentum is everything.
- Don't immediately pitch — ask one discovery question about their situation: "Before I put anything together, would it help to know more about what you're working on this season?"
- The goal is a 20-minute discovery call, not a full pitch deck. Keep the ladder short.

**For a "not right now" response:**
- "Totally understood — good to have the intro. I'll keep Profanity on your radar for when timing shifts." Short, no pressure.
- Add them to a light-touch nurture: a DM or email when something relevant happens (Profanity's work gets published, they launch something new, a shared industry moment).
- These leads often convert 6–12 months later.

**For no response:**
- Follow the follow-up sequence above
- After three touches with no response: one final note, then close the file and revisit in 6 months
- No guilt, no frustration. Timing is often the only variable.
