# SEO Reference — Profanity Creative

This is the working SEO reference for Dusty. It's adapted from a general-purpose audit framework and trimmed to what actually matters for a 3D product visualization studio's portfolio/blog site on Framer.

---

## Profanity's Keyword Map

### Primary (highest commercial intent)
- 3D product visualization
- Product CGI studio
- 3D product rendering
- Product visualization studio

### Secondary (good search volume, relevant audience)
- Outdoor product photography alternative
- Lifestyle brand CGI
- Action sports product rendering
- 3D product animation
- Product render studio Vermont
- Photorealistic product rendering

### Long-tail (lower volume, higher intent, easier to rank)
- CGI vs product photography for brands
- When to use CGI instead of photography
- 3D product visualization for outdoor brands
- How much does product CGI cost
- Product rendering for small brands

### Content-to-keyword mapping (existing posts)
Use these as the anchor SEO target for each piece:
- Blog posts → informational/comparison keywords ("CGI vs photography," "how product rendering works")
- Case studies → service + brand category ("3D product rendering [brand type]")
- About/services pages → primary commercial keywords ("3D product visualization studio")

## On-Page SEO Checklist

### Title Tags
- Primary keyword near the front of the title
- 50–60 characters (stays visible in Google results)
- Compelling enough to click — not just keyword + brand name
- Each page has a unique title
- Format: `Primary Keyword — Profanity Creative` or `How/Why [topic] | Profanity Creative`

### Meta Descriptions
- 150–160 characters
- Includes primary keyword naturally
- Has a clear reason to click (what will they get?)
- Not auto-generated or duplicated across pages
- Framer lets you set these per page — don't skip it

### H1 / Heading Structure
- One H1 per page
- H1 should contain or closely match the primary keyword
- Logical hierarchy: H1 → H2 → H3, no skipped levels
- Headings describe the actual content — not used just for visual styling
- Blog posts: H1 is the post title, H2s are major sections

### Content
- Primary keyword appears naturally in the first 100 words
- Related terms and synonyms woven in where they read naturally (not stuffed)
- Content answers what someone searching that keyword actually needs
- Minimum ~600 words for blog posts to signal depth; 1000+ for competitive topics
- No thin pages with little unique content

### Images
- File names are descriptive: `outdoor-brand-cgi-product-render.jpg` not `IMG_2034.jpg`
- Every image has alt text that describes what it shows
- Alt text on hero/key images should include the target keyword when it makes sense naturally
- Images are compressed (Framer handles this reasonably well but worth checking)

### Internal Linking
- Blog posts link to relevant case studies ("See how we did this for [brand type]")
- Case studies link back to relevant blog posts or services
- Services/about page links to case studies as proof
- Use descriptive anchor text — not "click here" or "read more," but "how we approached the FallTech render" or "our outdoor brand CGI work"

## E-E-A-T for a Creative Studio

E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust) matters more for service businesses than almost any other site type. Here's what it means for Profanity specifically:

**Experience** — Show the actual work. Specific client names (when permitted), real project details, actual results. "We rendered this product" is weak. "We built a full CGI library for FallTech's 2026 line because shooting harnesses on models at height was $40K they didn't have" is strong.

**Expertise** — Demonstrate knowledge of the outdoor/lifestyle space, not just CGI technique. Writing that shows you understand how brands in this space think, what they care about, and what problems they're trying to solve.

**Authoritativeness** — Get linked to from other industry sources. Guest posts, podcast appearances, mentions in outdoor industry publications, getting cited by brands. This is longer-term but worth thinking about.

**Trustworthiness** — Real contact info, real location (Vermont is actually an asset — it's authentic), real name and face behind the studio, privacy policy, HTTPS. Framer sites are HTTPS by default.

## Technical SEO (Framer-Specific)

Framer handles most of the technical basics well, but check these:

**Sitemap** — Framer auto-generates a sitemap. Make sure it's submitted to Google Search Console (console.google.com → Sitemaps).

**Robots.txt** — Framer generates this automatically. Verify no important pages are accidentally blocked.

**Core Web Vitals** — Check PageSpeed Insights (pagespeed.web.dev) for the site. Framer sites generally perform well but image-heavy pages can slow down. Common culprits: large uncompressed hero images, render-heavy animations.

**HTTPS** — Framer handles this automatically on custom domains.

**Mobile** — Framer is responsive by default, but always check that mobile layouts aren't hiding key content or breaking headings.

**Canonical tags** — Framer sets self-referencing canonicals automatically. If you ever duplicate a page or have similar content, make sure canonicals point to the right version.

**Page URLs** — Should be readable and keyword-relevant:
- Good: `/blog/cgi-vs-product-photography`
- Bad: `/blog/post-1` or `/blog/2024-03-15-untitled`

## SEO Audit Priority Order

When auditing any piece of content, work in this order:

1. **Title tag** — biggest single on-page ranking factor
2. **H1** — confirms topic to search engines
3. **Meta description** — affects click-through rate from results page
4. **Keyword in first 100 words** — confirms content relevance early
5. **Internal links** — distributes authority across the site
6. **Image alt text** — quick win, often overlooked
7. **Content depth** — does it actually answer the query?
8. **Heading structure** — readability and crawlability

## Common Issues on Studio/Portfolio Sites

- **Services pages are too thin** — "We do 3D product visualization" with one paragraph. Google won't rank this. Needs context, examples, process explanation, client outcomes.
- **Case studies have no text** — Just images and a client name. Add a paragraph about the brief, the challenge, and what was done. That's rankable content.
- **Blog posts don't link anywhere** — Orphaned content that doesn't connect to the rest of the site. Always link out to at least one relevant case study or services page.
- **No meta descriptions set** — Framer lets you set these per page. Takes 2 minutes. Do it.
- **Generic page titles** — "Home" or "Profanity Creative" as the title tag on the homepage. Should be "3D Product Visualization Studio | Profanity Creative."

## Tools (Free)

- **Google Search Console** — Track which keywords are driving impressions and clicks, spot indexation issues, submit sitemaps. Essential.
- **PageSpeed Insights** (pagespeed.web.dev) — Check Core Web Vitals per page.
- **Google Rich Results Test** — Verify schema markup if added.
- **Bing Webmaster Tools** — Secondary but worth setting up; covers the Bing/DuckDuckGo audience.
