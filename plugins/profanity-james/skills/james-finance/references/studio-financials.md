## Entity & Tax Treatment

- **Entity:** MFC Designs LLC d/b/a Profanity Creative, taxed as an S-Corporation
- **Owner-employee:** Mark Cernosia. Sole owner, sole employee.
- **How Mark gets paid:**
  - **W-2 wages** — a "reasonable salary" run through QB Payroll. As of 2026, the wage portion is roughly $4,150/month ($49,800 annualized). The CPA sets and reviews this number, not James.
  - **Distributions** — profit above wages flows through to Mark's personal return as K-1 income. Not deductible to the business. Not subject to self-employment tax (the S-Corp advantage). NOT covered by payroll withholding.
- **Federal tax structure:**
  - W-2 wages: federal income tax, FICA (Soc Sec + Medicare), FUTA. Withheld through payroll.
  - Distributions: subject to federal + state income tax on Mark's personal return, but NOT FICA. **Estimated quarterly payments owed** on this portion. This is the part most often missed.
- **Self-employment tax:** Does NOT apply to S-Corp distributions. Only FICA on the W-2 wage portion (employer + employee halves both paid through payroll). This is the main reason to be S-Corped.
- **Estimated taxes due quarterly** (for income tax owed on distributions, plus any income tax not covered by wage withholding):
  - April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15 (next year)
- **State:** Vermont. State income tax applies on both wages (withheld) and distributions (NOT withheld). VT also has its own estimated payment requirements.

## Reasonable Compensation — The S-Corp Rule James Doesn't Forget

The IRS requires S-Corp owner-employees to take a "reasonable salary" before taking distributions. If the salary is too low relative to the work performed, the IRS can reclassify distributions as wages and assess back FICA + penalties.

- **Why it matters:** Mark's salary number affects taxes both ways. Too high = more FICA paid, less S-Corp benefit. Too low = audit risk.
- **James's role:** Flag if wages drop dramatically relative to revenue, or if distributions vastly exceed wages over time. The actual number is the CPA's call.
- **Current baseline:** ~$49,800 annualized wages. Whatever distributions stack on top.

## IRS Installment Agreement (Currently Active)

Mark is on a payment plan for his 2024 taxes. It is almost paid off (as of Oct 2026). The plan is recorded in QuickBooks, which is the source of truth for the remaining balance and payment history. Auto-draft from M&T Bank:

- **Amount:** ~$1,947/month
- **Date:** Approximately the 11th of each month (verify in IRS online account)
- **Source:** Business checking (M&T MFC Designs)
- **Year-end:** these are personal income tax payments made from business checking. Track each one so the CPA can book it as a distribution.
- **Payoff:** when QuickBooks shows the balance at zero, drop the draft from fixed costs and the burn floor.

**Hard rule:** A missed/bounced auto-draft defaults the agreement. Once defaulted, the IRS can:
- Accelerate the full balance
- Issue a bank levy
- Begin collection actions
- Refuse to negotiate a new agreement for some period

**James treats the auto-draft as untouchable.** Cash management always prioritizes covering this draft. If the draft date is approaching and the account looks thin, that is a "stop everything else and address this" event.

## Cash Reserves

- **Tax reserve for distribution income:** Mark should be parking a % of distributions in a separate account to cover the federal + VT income tax owed on K-1 income that's NOT covered by payroll withholding. The CPA picks the exact %, but a rough working number is 20-30% of distributions.
- **Operating reserve:** Industry rule of thumb is 3-6 months of fixed costs. For a one-person S-Corp running ~$10K/mo, the floor is ~$30K. Below that, every slow month becomes a crisis.
- **Emergency / opportunity:** Anything above operating reserve.
