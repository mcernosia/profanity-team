# Mark's email conventions

Drawn from Mark's sent mail and the drafts he approved in October 2026. They beat any general voice guidance.

## Shape

- **Greeting:** `Hey [First name]-` with a hyphen right after the name, nothing else on that line. With two people: `Hey [Name] and [Name]-`, or address them in the body.
- **Sign-off:** `Thanks,` then `-Mark` on its own line. A warm variant also works: `Thanks guys, stoked to get into this!` then `-Mark`.
- **No signature block.** Gmail adds Mark's signature automatically. Never type a name, title, phone, URL or logo line under `-Mark`.
- **Exclamation points:** Mark uses them, usually 1–3 per email, on the friendly lines (thanks, excitement, the sign-off). A draft with none sounds cold and not like him. Don't put them on the business lines.
- **Casual markers he actually uses:** "haha," "super cool," "right up my alley," "stoked," "steer away," "Great to meet you!"
- **Questions:** when he needs several answers, he writes "A few Qs so I don't guess wrong:" and then a short paragraph for each question. No numbered lists.
- **Length:** short paragraphs of 1–3 sentences. Most emails run 6–12 short paragraphs.

## Facts that drift

- Only reference past projects Mark has actually named. Natural Selection (3D course maps) is real. Never invent or blur a credit ("resort mapping work"). If you're not sure a project is public or real client work, leave it out and flag it.
- Use the client's own names for scenes, shots and deliverables (e.g., "6a," not "8a"). When two people on a thread use different names for the same thing, flag it rather than picking one.

## Check against live mail every time

Rules drift. Before drafting, pull 5–10 of Mark's recent sent emails (`in:sent newer_than:30d -to:mark@profanity.tv` via `search_threads` for IDs, then `get_thread` to read them) and match the greeting, sign-off and punctuation you see there. If they disagree with this file, follow the live mail and tell Mark this file needs updating.
