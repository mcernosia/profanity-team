# Profanity Creative — Brand Voice Reference

## The Core Tension

Profanity makes work that's technically precise and visually stunning. But the studio's personality is anything but corporate. That tension — craft without pretension — is the whole brand. Copy should live in that space.

Mark isn't performing humility. He's genuinely more interested in the work than the marketing. That comes through. Don't overwrite it.

---

## Voice Characteristics

### Direct
Say the thing. No warm-up sentences. No "we're thrilled to share." No "excited to announce." Just: here's the work, here's why it matters.

### Confident
The work is good. Mark knows it. Don't hedge. Don't undersell. Don't add disclaimers. Present it like it belongs.

### Slightly Irreverent
A dry sense of humor is welcome. A light self-awareness about the work (or about how people talk about the work) can land well. Never try-hard. Never forced. If the joke doesn't come naturally, skip it.

### Never Corporate
No press release language. No buzzwords. No "leveraging our expertise to deliver best-in-class solutions." If a sentence could appear in a brand deck, rewrite it.

### Vermont / Studio Authenticity
There's a quiet DIY energy to the studio. It's not a big agency. It's a small operation that punches above its weight. That's a selling point. Don't hide it or dress it up.

---

## Do's

- Start with the work. What are we looking at? What makes this specific render, shot, or angle interesting?
- Use specific details. "The frost condensation on the bottle neck" beats "product detail."
- Reference the actual client or product when relevant (and approved). Specificity signals credibility.
- Let silence do work. A short caption with a clean image is often stronger than a paragraph.
- Write like Mark is talking to someone he respects — a peer in the industry, not a potential customer he's pitching.

---

## Don'ts

- "Excited/thrilled/honored/proud to share…" — delete on sight.
- Vague superlatives: "stunning," "incredible," "next-level" — if you can't say *why* it's good, don't say it.
- Hashtag stuffing with irrelevant tags — quality over quantity.
- Tagging brands who aren't involved in the project just for reach — looks desperate.
- Posting the same caption on both Instagram and LinkedIn — they serve different audiences and they'll feel the difference.
- Over-explaining the process. Save that for behind-the-scenes posts. The hero shot doesn't need a tech breakdown.
- "We" language when it's just Mark. He can say "I" — it's actually more authentic.

---

## Example Copy — Good vs. Bad

### Instagram

**Bad:**
"Thrilled to share this stunning product visualization for our latest client project! Our team really pushed the boundaries of what's possible in 3D rendering. So much detail went into every frame. We're incredibly proud of how this turned out. #3D #CGI #ProductViz #Design"

**Good:**
"Spent a week on the ice texture alone. Worth it.
Shot for @[client] — full project on the site.
#3drendering #productvisualization #outdoorgear #cgi"

---

### LinkedIn

**Bad:**
"Excited to announce the completion of another successful project for a leading outdoor lifestyle brand! Leveraging our expertise in photorealistic 3D visualization, we delivered a suite of assets that exceeded client expectations. Looking forward to future collaborations!"

**Good:**
"Just wrapped a campaign for [client]. They needed shots that could carry the full campaign — hero images, e-commerce, social — without a single photo shoot.

Saved them a trip to Iceland. Delivered assets they're still using a year later.

That's the case for 3D. Not cheaper. More versatile."

---

## Tone Calibration by Platform

| | Instagram | LinkedIn |
|---|---|---|
| Tone | Personal, visual, dry | Professional but human |
| Length | 1–3 sentences | 3–6 sentences |
| Goal | Stop the scroll | Make the business case |
| Audience | Creatives, enthusiasts, peers | Decision-makers, buyers |
| Voice | Mark talking to peers | Mark in a meeting, but honest |

---

## Words/Phrases to Use

- "Spent [time] on [detail]." — conveys craft without bragging
- "Worth it." — simple, confident
- "The brief was [X]. We did [Y]." — shows problem-solving
- "Full project on the site." — low-key CTA
- "Shot for [client]." — clean credit
- Specific material or lighting references when they serve a point

## Words/Phrases to Avoid

- Thrilled, excited, honored, proud to share
- Next-level, stunning, incredible, breathtaking
- Leverage, deliver, solutions, expertise
- "We exceeded expectations"
- "Best-in-class"
- Any sentence starting with "As a studio..."
